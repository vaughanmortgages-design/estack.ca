How to Raise Your Credit Score in Canada: 7 Proven Steps

Updated August 2026  ·  7 min read  ·  estack.ca editorial team

If you want to improve or raise your credit score in Canada, focus on the few factors that actually move a Canadian credit file: payment history, revolving utilization, account age, credit mix, and new inquiries.

Canada uses credit scores that generally range from 300 to 900. There is no single score that guarantees approval, and every lender has its own underwriting rules, but higher scores can improve access to mainstream credit products.

7 Steps to Improve Your Credit Score in Canada

1

Pay Every Bill On Time

Payment history is one of the strongest credit factors. Set up automatic minimum payments so a forgotten due date does not turn into a late payment.

2

Lower Credit Card Utilization

If a card has a $10,000 limit, keeping the reported balance below $3,000 puts utilization below 30%. Lower is generally better. Paying before the statement date can help reduce the balance that gets reported.

3

Keep Older Accounts Open When They Still Make Sense

Older accounts can support the length of your credit history. Do not keep an expensive account only for age, but avoid closing useful no-fee accounts without considering the impact on utilization and history.

4

Limit Unnecessary Hard Inquiries

Applications for new credit can create hard inquiries. Apply only when needed and avoid opening several accounts in a short period.

5

Check Equifax and TransUnion for Errors

Review both Canadian credit reports. If an account, balance, late payment, or personal detail is wrong, use the bureau's dispute process and keep documentation.

6

Use a Secured Card if Your Credit File Is Thin

A secured credit card can help build payment history when traditional credit is difficult to obtain. Choose one that reports to the Canadian credit bureaus and pay it on time.

7

Keep Balances Predictable

Avoid maxing out cards even if you pay them in full later. Stable, low reported balances make utilization easier to manage month after month.

Realistic timeline: Some changes can show within one to three billing cycles. A larger rebuild, including a possible 100-point increase, often takes many months and depends on where you start and what is on your reports.

Can You Increase Your Credit Score by 100 Points in Canada?

It can happen, but there is no guaranteed formula. The fastest improvements usually come from correcting reporting errors or sharply reducing high credit-card utilization. If negative items are accurate, improvement is usually gradual as newer positive history accumulates.

Credit Score Ranges: What Matters Most

Instead of chasing one magic number, focus on the full credit profile. Lenders may also consider income, debt-service ratios, down payment or collateral, recent applications, and the type of credit you are seeking.

Bottom Line

To raise your credit score in Canada, pay on time, keep utilization low, avoid unnecessary inquiries, preserve useful older accounts, and check both bureaus for errors. Consistency matters more than shortcuts.
