Gold vs Silver, Coins vs Bars, and Storage/Delivery Tradeoffs

Gold vs Silver

Gold is often chosen for compact long-term value storage, while silver is commonly used for lower per-unit entry points and smaller transaction sizes.

Coins vs Bars

Coins can be easier to identify and resell in portions; bars may be more premium-efficient in larger sizes but are less divisible.

Storage Priority

Choose storage before ordering: home safe, bank box, or allocated vault. Confirm access rules, insurance scope and documentation.

Delivery Priority

Check tracked shipping, insurance, signature requirements, packaging discretion and claims process before final checkout.

Understand the Premium Over Spot

The spot price is the market reference price for gold. A retail buyer normally pays more than spot because the final price can include fabrication, dealer margin, payment processing, shipping and insurance.

Smaller coins and bars often carry a higher percentage premium than larger products. A lower advertised premium is not automatically the best deal if payment or shipping fees are added later.

Payment, Delivery and Verification

  • Payment: Confirm whether card, bank transfer or other payment methods change the final price.
  • Delivery: Check whether shipping is tracked, insured and signature-required, and understand the claims process.
  • Product details: Verify weight, purity, mint or refiner, packaging and any serial-number or assay information.
  • Seller: Review established history, clear contact details, policies and independent reputation signals.
  • Records: Keep invoices, confirmations and product documentation for insurance, resale and tax records.

Storage & Delivery Checklist

  • Confirm insured shipping and signature requirements before payment.
  • Verify how damaged or missing shipments are handled.
  • Decide where metal will be stored before ordering.
  • Keep invoices and product details for insurance and resale records.

Storage Options in Canada

Home safe

Consider physical security, discreet installation, access controls and whether your insurer specifically covers bullion. Do not assume standard home coverage is sufficient.

Bank safe-deposit box

Ask about access hours, box availability and insurance. Contents are generally not the same as an insured bank deposit.

Professional vault storage

Confirm whether storage is allocated or unallocated, how ownership is documented, insurance coverage, audit practices, access and withdrawal fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should Canadians compare before buying gold bullion?

Compare the total delivered cost, the premium over spot, payment fees, shipping and insurance terms, storage options, product authenticity information and the dealer's return or buyback policy.

Are gold coins or gold bars better for a first purchase?

Coins are often easier to recognize and resell in smaller amounts, while bars may have lower premiums at larger sizes. The better choice depends on budget, resale flexibility and storage preference.

How should physical gold be stored in Canada?

Common options include a secured home safe, a bank safe-deposit box or professional allocated vault storage. Verify access, insurance, fees and whether the metal is specifically allocated in your name.
