Understand the Premium Over Spot

The spot price is the market reference price for gold. A retail buyer normally pays more than spot because the final price can include fabrication, dealer margin, payment processing, shipping and insurance.

Smaller coins and bars often carry a higher percentage premium than larger products. A lower advertised premium is not automatically the best deal if payment or shipping fees are added later.

Payment, Delivery and Verification

  • Payment: Confirm whether card, bank transfer or other payment methods change the final price.
  • Delivery: Check whether shipping is tracked, insured and signature-required.
  • Product details: Verify weight, purity, mint or refiner, packaging and assay information.
  • Seller: Review established history, clear contact details and policies.
  • Records: Keep invoices and product documentation.

Storage & Delivery Checklist

  • Confirm insured shipping and signature requirements before payment.
  • Verify how damaged or missing shipments are handled.
  • Decide where the metal will be stored before ordering.
  • Keep invoices and product details for insurance and resale records.

Storage Options in Canada

Home safe

Consider physical security, discreet installation, access controls and whether your insurer specifically covers bullion.

Bank safe-deposit box

Ask about access hours, box availability and insurance. Contents are generally not the same as an insured bank deposit.

Professional vault storage

Confirm whether storage is allocated or unallocated, how ownership is documented, insurance coverage, audit practices and withdrawal fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should Canadians compare before buying gold bullion?

Compare the total delivered cost, premium over spot, payment fees, shipping and insurance terms, storage options, authenticity information and the dealer's return or buyback policy.

Are gold coins or gold bars better for a first purchase?

Coins are often easier to recognize and resell in smaller amounts, while bars may have lower premiums at larger sizes.

How should physical gold be stored in Canada?

Common options include a secured home safe, bank safe-deposit box or professional allocated vault storage.
