Gold Bullion Buying Checklist

1. Compare the all-in cost

Add the premium over spot, payment fees, shipping, insurance and storage charges.

2. Verify the product

Confirm weight, purity, mint or refiner, packaging and assay or authenticity details.

3. Confirm delivery

Check tracking, insurance, signature requirements and procedures for damaged or missing shipments.

4. Review resale terms

Understand the dealer’s current buyback process, spreads, identification requirements and settlement timing.

5. Decide on storage

Choose between a secured home safe, safe-deposit box or professional allocated vault storage.

6. Keep records

Save invoices, product descriptions, serial numbers and storage or insurance documentation.

Gold Bullion Premiums and Fees

The spot price is a market reference. Retail buyers normally pay above spot because the final price can include fabrication, dealer margin, payment processing, shipping and insurance. Smaller bars and coins often carry a higher percentage premium than larger products.

A lower displayed premium does not automatically mean a lower total price. Compare the final checkout amount for the same product size and payment method.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can Canadians buy gold bullion?

Canadians can buy from established online dealers, local bullion and coin dealers, selected financial institutions and precious-metals marketplaces. Compare the total delivered cost and seller policies.

Are gold bars or gold coins better?

Bars may be more premium-efficient at larger sizes. Recognized coins may be easier to resell in smaller amounts. The better choice depends on budget, storage and resale flexibility.

Is gold bullion taxable in Canada?

Certain qualifying precious metals may receive different sales-tax treatment than collectible or non-qualifying products. Confirm the treatment of the exact product with the dealer or a qualified tax professional.

How do I verify a bullion dealer?

Review established business history, clear contact information, product descriptions, shipping and return policies, authentication details and current buyback terms.

How should physical gold be stored?

Common choices include a secured home safe, bank safe-deposit box and professional allocated vault storage. Compare access, insurance, fees and ownership documentation.
