Bank of Canada Rate Decision, October 28, 2026: What It Means for Your Money

Published October 2026 · estack.ca editorial team
Direct answer: The Bank of Canada announces its next rate decision on Wednesday, October 28, 2026. Going in, the policy rate sits at 2.25% and prime at the major lenders is 4.45%. A hold leaves variable mortgage and HELOC payments exactly where they are. A cut of 0.25% would typically pull prime down to 4.20% and lower the cost of anything priced off prime. Nobody knows the decision in advance — this page walks through both outcomes so you are ready either way.

The October 28 announcement is the one that sets borrowing costs heading into the end of the year. If you hold a variable-rate mortgage, carry a HELOC balance, face a renewal this winter, or have cash in a GIC or savings account, the decision touches your money directly. Here is the plain breakdown.

Where Rates Stand Right Now

Prime moves in step with the policy rate — historically prime equals the policy rate plus 2.20%. That is why a single announcement changes payments for millions of Canadians within one billing cycle. For the full picture of current Canadian rates, see our Canadian interest rates page.

If the Bank Holds

A hold is the status quo, and it cuts both ways. Variable mortgage payments and HELOC interest stay flat — no relief, but no new pressure either. Anyone renewing this winter keeps negotiating against the same rate environment they have today.

For savers, a hold is quietly good news: new GIC rates and high-interest savings rates tend to stay steadier when the policy rate does not move. If you have cash to lock in, a hold means the offers on the table today are roughly the offers you will still see next month. Compare terms on our GIC rates guide and HISA guide.

If the Bank Cuts

A cut — most likely 0.25% if it comes — lowers prime to about 4.20%. Here is what that means in practice:

  • Variable-rate mortgages: your rate drops by the same amount. With an adjustable-rate mortgage the payment falls; with a fixed-payment variable mortgage, more of each payment goes to principal instead.
  • HELOCs: interest on your drawn balance gets cheaper right away, since HELOCs are priced at prime or prime plus a margin.
  • Fixed-rate mortgages: no direct change. Fixed rates follow bond yields, which price in expectations — a widely expected cut may already be reflected in today's fixed offers.
  • GICs and savings: new GIC rates and promotional HISA rates usually drift down in the weeks after a cut. GICs you already hold keep their locked rate until maturity.
What is 0.25% worth? Example only: on a $100,000 balance priced off prime, a 0.25% cut saves roughly $250 of interest over a full year — about $21 a month — assuming the balance and rate stay the same. Scale it to your own balance to get your number.

Renewing This Winter?

The decision matters most if your mortgage renews in the next few months. A hold or a cut before your renewal date shapes the offers lenders put in front of you, and the fixed-versus-variable choice gets harder when rates are mid-move. Our variable vs fixed mortgage guide walks through that decision, and our earlier explainer on what a rate hold means for your mortgage covers the renewal playbook in detail.

One point that does not change with the decision: start the renewal conversation about 120 days out, not 30. That window is where the leverage is, whatever the Bank does on October 28.

Before October 28 — a short checklist

  • Confirm whether your mortgage is variable or fixed, and what it is priced off.
  • Check your HELOC rate and drawn balance.
  • Note your renewal date; if it falls before spring, start comparing offers now.
  • If you hold GICs, check maturity dates — a cut makes early renewal offers less generous.
  • If your savings sit in a promotional HISA, note when the promotion ends.
  • Re-check this page and the rates page after the announcement for the updated prime rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the Bank of Canada announcement?

Wednesday, October 28, 2026. The Bank announces its policy rate eight times a year on a published schedule.

What is the current policy rate and prime rate?

Going into the October 28 decision, the policy rate is 2.25% and prime at the major lenders is 4.45%.

Will a cut lower my fixed mortgage payment?

No. A fixed payment stays fixed until renewal. A cut affects variable mortgages and HELOCs directly; fixed rates respond to bond yields, not the announcement itself.

Should I lock in a GIC before the decision?

If you were planning to lock in anyway and you are worried about a cut, doing it before October 28 secures today's rate. If the Bank holds, little changes. Either way, compare terms first and do not rush a long lock-in purely on a guess about one announcement.

Bottom Line

Hold or cut, the October 28 decision is a planning event, not a surprise to fear. A hold keeps everything exactly as it is today: policy rate 2.25%, prime 4.45%. A cut makes prime-based borrowing cheaper and new savings rates less generous. Know which side of that line your money sits on before the announcement, and the decision becomes a number you planned for instead of a headline you react to.
