Bank Said No? Here's What to Do Next

estack.caJune 20267 min read

A declined loan or mortgage application does not always end your financing options. The right next step depends on the reason for the decision, the amount you need, your income and credit, and your ability to repay. Alternative financing can cost more, and approval is never guaranteed.

Why Banks Say No to Good Borrowers

Canada's major banks use rigid qualification criteria that penalize perfectly capable borrowers. Self-employed income, T4 gaps, recent career changes, credit issues from years ago, or simply a complicated income structure can all trigger a decline — even if you have the income and assets to service the loan comfortably.

Step 1 — Understand Why You Were Declined

Ask the lender which factors contributed to the decision and request whatever explanation they can provide. A credit issue calls for a different response than insufficient income documentation, high debt payments or property-related concerns.

Step 2 — Pull Your Credit Report

In Canada, review your credit information with Equifax Canada and TransUnion Canada. Look for mistakes, unfamiliar accounts and outdated information, and use the bureau's dispute process where appropriate. Corrections and score changes do not happen on a guaranteed timetable.

Step 3 — Talk to a Mortgage Broker, Not Another Bank

A licensed mortgage broker or agent can review your situation and explain which lender categories may be worth considering, including some alternative or private options. Interest rates, lender fees, broker fees and repayment risks may be higher. Ask about credit checks before submitting additional applications.

Step 4 — If You Need Time, Build Your File

If you're 6–18 months away from being ready, use that time strategically:

  • Pay down revolving credit below 30% utilization
  • Avoid unnecessary new credit applications while preparing your file; credit checks can affect scores, depending on the circumstances
  • If self-employed, ensure your last two NOAs show consistent or growing income
  • Save the largest down payment you can manage
